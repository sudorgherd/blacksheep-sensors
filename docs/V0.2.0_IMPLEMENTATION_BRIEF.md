# v0.2.0 Raw Sensor Attribute Discovery — Implementation Brief

**Status:** Approved implementation plan; firmware work not started
**Baseline:** BLACKSHEEP Sensors v0.1.0, ESP-IDF 5.5.4, ESP32-C5 revision 1.2
**Milestone boundary:** characterize candidate attributes; do not define the
v0.3.0 observation model

## 1. Executive summary

v0.2.0 will determine which Wi-Fi attributes the two ESP32-C5 sensors can
extract reliably, under what conditions they are meaningful, and what their
bounded processing cost is. It will add a length-safe, host-testable 802.11
header parser and controlled discovery firmware, then exercise both assigned
bands with ambient and controlled traffic. Every candidate attribute will end
the milestone with an evidence-backed disposition: **Accept**, **Accept with
conditions**, **Experimental**, **Defer**, or **Reject**.

This milestone is deliberately not a product-data design exercise. Its parsed
frame structure is a test instrument, not the BLACKSHEEP sensor record. It will
not establish retention, aggregation, transport, correlation, clustering,
fingerprinting, or a control-board protocol. Those decisions begin with the
v0.3.0 observation model and later milestones.

The recommended implementation separates capture from interpretation:

```text
ESP-IDF promiscuous callback
  -> validate callback class and advertised/captured lengths
  -> copy normalized RX metadata + at most 40 header bytes
  -> non-blocking fixed-capacity internal-RAM queue
  -> worker task performs length-safe parsing and bounded characterization
  -> summary/disposition evidence (never payload or pcap retention)
```

The initial engineering bounds are 40 copied 802.11 bytes, 128 queue entries,
zero wait in the callback, explicit enqueue/drop/high-water counters, and
finite eight-second capture windows. Measurements in Stage 1 may reduce these
bounds or justify a documented increase; no buffer may become unbounded.

## 2. Current baseline

v0.1.0 physically established:

* two Waveshare boards using ESP32-C5-WROOM-1U-N32R8 modules;
* C5 #1 assigned to 2.4 GHz and C5 #2 assigned to 5 GHz;
* ESP-IDF 5.5.4 through the pinned PlatformIO environment;
* 32 MB Quad-SPI flash, 8 MB Quad-SPI PSRAM, and stable boot on both boards;
* the project-local native-CMake bootloader selection and regression guard;
* fixed-band scanning, promiscuous callbacks, and deterministic channel control;
* a direct C5 receive-control inventory on fixed channels on both bands.

The authoritative direct-driver findings are in
[`V0.1.0_C5_WIFI_METADATA.md`](V0.1.0_C5_WIFI_METADATA.md). They show that
callback class, RSSI, channel, noise floor, timestamp, lengths, group indication,
receive state, baseband format, conditional rate, and raw SIG fields reach the
public callback. They also show that addresses, frame subtype, sequence/retry,
and protected state require raw 802.11 header parsing. No v0.1.0 test read or
retained the payload buffer.

All new firmware environments must continue using `scripts/native_bootloader.py`
and its SHA/header guard. Existing v0.1.0 environments and diagnostics are
regression requirements, not code to redesign during attribute discovery.

## 3. Objective and decision questions

v0.2.0 is an **attribute discovery and characterization** milestone. For each
candidate it must answer:

1. Is the value directly available or safely derivable on ESP32-C5?
2. For which callback classes, 802.11 layouts, bands, and PHY formats is it valid?
3. Are the semantics documented, inferred, or still physically unverified?
4. What CPU, callback-time, queue, and memory cost does extraction impose?
5. Is it meaningful when transmitters use locally administered/randomized MACs?
6. Can it be processed transiently without keeping raw frames or payloads?
7. Is behavior consistent enough across 2.4 and 5 GHz to inform v0.3.0?
8. Which physical evidence is required before it can advance?

The milestone may expose bounded diagnostic summaries and controlled known-test
identifiers. It must not freeze a field name, wire encoding, storage duration,
or grouping key for the future sensor record.

## 4. Scope

In scope:

* public ESP-IDF promiscuous receive-control metadata;
* safe parsing of only the 802.11 MAC header bytes required by this inventory;
* type/subtype, flag, sequence-control, and address-presence discovery;
* conditional address-role resolution where IEEE 802.11 layout permits it;
* local/group/global MAC-bit classification;
* channel-to-band/frequency derivation with an explicit regulatory context;
* bounded RSSI, noise, length, timestamp, activity, and PHY-format experiments;
* controlled traffic and host fixtures needed to validate the above;
* evidence-backed candidate dispositions and documented limitations.

## 5. Explicit non-goals

v0.2.0 will not implement or decide:

* the final observation schema, identifier, serialization, or retention policy;
* persistent capture, pcap output, payload parsing, decryption, or content analysis;
* a production OUI database or manufacturer feature;
* device identity, fingerprinting, clustering, correlation, or behavior inference;
* long-term aggregation, activity profiles, or adaptive channel scheduling;
* cross-C5 time synchronization or simultaneous dual-board integration;
* a control-board/UART interface, REDLINE transport, BLE, GNSS, or CSI sensing;
* private ESP-IDF ABI dependencies or reverse-engineered driver state.

## 6. Technical authority and assumptions

Primary authority is the pinned ESP-IDF 5.5.4 source installed by the existing
build. Relevant paths, relative to the ESP-IDF root, are:

* `components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi.h` — promiscuous callback and control APIs;
* `components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi_types_generic.h` — callback packet types
  and filter masks;
* `components/esp_wifi/include/local/esp_wifi_types_native.h` —
  `wifi_promiscuous_pkt_t` and the C5 receive-control alias;
* `components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi_he_types.h` — the packed
  `esp_wifi_rxctrl_t` used by C5 (`SOC_WIFI_MAC_VERSION_NUM == 3`);
* `examples/network/simple_sniffer/main/cmd_sniffer.c` — an Espressif example of
  copying callback data into a queue for later processing.

IEEE 802.11 MAC header layout is the authority for raw-frame semantics. During
implementation, constants and fixtures must cite the standard edition or a
public Espressif definition used; comments must distinguish standard-defined
layout from project inference. The implementation must not include copied
private driver structures merely because they reveal more fields.

Documented facts:

* `WIFI_PKT_MGMT`, `WIFI_PKT_CTRL`, and `WIFI_PKT_DATA` callbacks use
  `wifi_promiscuous_pkt_t`; `WIFI_PKT_MISC` has a zero-length payload.
* `payload` is a zero-length trailing member and its content is selected by the
  callback packet type; `rx_ctrl.sig_len` describes the reception MPDU.
* the C5 public RX-control structure is packed and exposes the fields inventoried
  in v0.1.0;
* timestamp is local microseconds and is precise only when modem/light sleep is
  disabled;
* `rate` interpretation depends on `cur_bb_format`; SIG words are raw.

The public headers do not promise that the callback buffer remains valid after
the callback returns. The design therefore treats it as callback-lifetime-only:
required bytes and normalized metadata are copied before return, and no pointer
escapes. This is a conservative implementation inference to be verified against
the matching programming guide/release notes during Stage 1.

Physical results from a short window demonstrate existence, not universal PHY
coverage or reliability. US regulatory settings, fixed role/band, and the
verified channel at capture time are part of every test record.

## 7. ESP32-C5 input surface

### 7.1 Promiscuous callback contract

The callback receives `void *buf` and `wifi_promiscuous_pkt_type_t type`.
For management, control, and data classes, cast only after checking `buf` and
`type`. For miscellaneous callbacks, record a class count if desired but never
read payload bytes. Reject unsuccessful receptions (`rx_state != 0` or
`rxend_state != 0`) from semantic parsing while retaining bounded error counts.

`sig_len` and `dump_len` require focused validation: v0.1.0 observed
`dump_len == sig_len + 4` despite the public comments describing `dump_len` as
excluding FCS. Until resolved, use the smaller plausible captured length for
copy bounds, never add four bytes speculatively, and classify length/FCS meaning
as ambiguous. Stage 1 must use controlled frames of known over-the-air length
and guards around every copy.

### 7.2 Direct receive-control fields

The public C5 structure supplies: RSSI, conditional rate, four interface-match
bits, raw SIG-A words, reception end state, group indication, 32-bit timestamp,
noise floor, SIG-B length, primary and secondary channel, baseband format,
channel-estimate length/valid flag, MPDU length fields, and receive state.
There is no directly decoded per-packet band, MCS, coding, aggregation, STBC,
guard interval, or antenna field in this C5 structure.

Band is controlled context, verified with `esp_wifi_get_band_mode()` and the
configured channel rather than inferred from an absent per-packet field.
Frequency is a derived value and must preserve the source channel and regulatory
context. The initial supported mapping is US 2.4 GHz channels 1–11 and the
already qualified non-DFS 5 GHz channels 36, 40, 44, and 48. Channel 14's
special frequency and 6 GHz numbering are outside the initial mapper; unknown
inputs return unknown rather than a plausible-looking frequency.

### 7.3 Raw frame surface

Only the MAC-header prefix needed for safe discovery will be copied. No frame
body, information element, encrypted payload, LLC/SNAP content, or FCS is
retained. Callback packet class is useful as a driver assertion, while the raw
frame-control type is parsed independently; mismatches are counted and treated
as evidence of malformed, truncated, or misunderstood input.

## 9. Minimum safe 802.11 parser design

### 9.1 Separation and interfaces

Implement pure, dependency-light parser code in a new library/component rather
than embedding byte offsets in `main.c`. A conceptual API is:

```c
parse_status_t wifi_mac_parse(const uint8_t *bytes, size_t captured_len,
                              parsed_mac_header_t *out);
```

`parsed_mac_header_t` is bounded and validity-driven: numeric fields plus
`has_*` flags, four six-byte address slots, resolved role indices (or unknown),
computed header length, and a parse-status/error enum. It contains no pointers
to callback storage, frame body, dynamic allocation, vendor name, history, or
transport/schema concern. It is specifically not a v0.3.0 record.

### 9.2 Byte order and access discipline

802.11 MAC multi-byte fields used here are little-endian. Read them explicitly
from bytes (`p[0] | p[1] << 8`) rather than casting packed structs, relying on
host endianness, alignment, or C bit-field layout. Before every read, prove
`captured_len >= offset + width`. Initialize output to an all-invalid state;
partial parsing never leaves stale validity bits.

The parser order is:

1. require two bytes, decode FC protocol/type/subtype/flags;
2. reject unsupported protocol versions without further semantic parsing;
3. choose a type/subtype layout descriptor;
4. calculate the minimum required header length using overflow-safe constants;
5. require that length before copying any address or control field;
6. decode only fields declared present by the descriptor;
7. resolve roles from type/subtype and DS bits; unresolved roles stay unknown;
8. return success, truncated, unsupported, malformed, or ambiguous-layout status.

### 9.3 Header layouts

No global 24-byte assumption is allowed.

* Management frames normally require the 24-byte base MAC header: FC, duration,
  Addr1, Addr2, Addr3, and Sequence Control. A documented subtype-specific
  extension is parsed only after its own length check.
* Control frames use subtype descriptors. ACK and CTS have a short receiver-only
  layout; RTS and several other subtypes include receiver plus transmitter or
  subtype-specific address roles; Block Ack Request/Block Ack and Control
  Wrapper have their own minimums. Unknown/reserved control subtypes yield FC
  data only and `unsupported_subtype`, never a fabricated 24-byte header.
* Data frames start with the 24-byte three-address layout. When To DS and From
  DS are both set, Addr4 extends it to 30 bytes. QoS data subtypes add a two-byte
  QoS Control field. Where the standard-defined Order/HT-Control condition is
  met, add four more bytes. Each addition is checked before access.

The initial 40-byte prefix covers the largest header needed for these discovery
fields with margin. It is not a claim that every 802.11 header/body is 40 bytes.
Frames needing more bytes for a future experiment return an explicit
`insufficient_capture`/`unsupported_extension` result.

### 9.4 Malformed and truncated behavior

Truncation, impossible length relations, unknown protocol versions, callback/FC
class mismatch, unsupported subtypes, and invalid channel context increment
separate saturating counters. They produce no address roles and never panic,
read beyond the copied prefix, or log raw bytes. Fuzz/property tests should run
every input length from zero through the maximum prefix and randomized contents
under AddressSanitizer/UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer on the host where available.

## 10. Address-role semantics

Raw `AddrN` and semantic roles are distinct. The parser records address presence
first, then resolves roles only under a standard-defined layout.

### 10.1 Management frames

For ordinary management frames with the complete base header:

* Addr1 = receiver/destination;
* Addr2 = transmitter/source;
* Addr3 = BSSID, subject to subtype-specific special cases documented in the
  parser table.

The implementation must keep subtype-specific exceptions explicit. It must not
apply this mapping to control or data frames merely because 24 bytes exist.

### 10.2 Data frames

| To DS | From DS | Addr1 | Addr2 | Addr3 | Addr4 | Resolvable roles |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | RA/DA | TA/SA | BSSID | absent | receiver, destination, transmitter, source, BSSID |
| 1 | 0 | RA/BSSID | TA/SA | DA | absent | receiver, BSSID, transmitter, source, destination |
| 0 | 1 | RA/DA | TA/BSSID | SA | absent | receiver, destination, transmitter, BSSID, source |
| 1 | 1 | RA | TA | DA | SA | receiver, transmitter, destination, source; BSSID unknown |

IBSS uses the 0/0 data mapping with Addr3 as BSSID. Four-address/WDS frames do
not get a guessed BSSID. Null-data and QoS-null frames still have address fields
but no data payload; that distinction is recorded by subtype, not inferred from
length.

### 10.3 Control frames

Control roles are subtype-specific. ACK/CTS expose receiver only. RTS exposes
receiver and transmitter. PS-Poll and CF-End variants give certain address
fields protocol-specific BSSID/TA or RA/BSSID meanings; Block Ack control frames
have their documented RA/TA layout. Source/destination/BSSID remain unknown
unless the subtype definition supports those exact roles. Reserved or incomplete
control frames expose only safely decoded FC data.

### 10.4 Group and unknown handling

The I/G bit is meaningful on a present destination/receiver address; all `FF`
is broadcast, other I/G-set values are multicast/group. A group address is not
eligible for OUI attribution. If any role cannot be resolved, its validity flag
is false—zero bytes are never used as an “unknown MAC.” Host tests cover every
DS mapping, short control form, four-address frame, broadcast, multicast, and
ambiguous subtype.

## 11. Randomized MAC and OUI strategy

For an individual MAC address, bit 1 of the first octet is the U/L bit: set means
locally administered; clear means globally administered. Bit 0 is the I/G bit:
set means group. Classification order must be: validate presence, detect
broadcast/group, then local/global. A locally administered value may be stable,
rotating, randomized, or manually configured; the bit alone does not prove which.

OUI lookup is meaningful only as a cautious prefix-assignment hint for eligible
globally administered individual addresses. It can still identify the interface
vendor rather than the product maker and can be stale, reassigned, virtualized,
or absent. Applying an ordinary OUI label to a local/randomized or group address
is misleading and prohibited.

**Recommendation:** do not put a manufacturer database on either C5 in v0.2.0.
Implement and host-test eligibility plus 24-bit prefix extraction. Test lookup
feasibility on the host with a tiny fixture table containing known positive,
unknown, local, and group cases. Defer the production database, update policy,
storage cost, and execution location to v0.3.0 or later. This keeps C5 callback
work bounded and prevents a rapidly stale database from becoming firmware.

Controlled serial diagnostics should normally print classifications and counts,
not addresses. A temporary compile-time test mode may print only a small capped
number (recommended maximum 16 per run) of addresses belonging to explicitly
known lab devices, with no persistence. The default and ambient-RF modes must
not print identifiers.

## 12. RSSI and RF metadata strategy

RSSI and noise floor are observations, not calibrated range estimates. Antenna,
orientation, multipath, transmit power, PHY, channel, enclosure, and board-to-
board variation can dominate. v0.2.0 should determine whether they are stable
and contextual enough to advance, not perform v0.7.0 calibration.

Required experiment:

* use one known AP/client per band on a fixed verified channel;
* collect at least 10 finite windows at each of three repeatable placements
  (near, middle, far), with at least 100 eligible frames per placement where
  traffic permits;
* repeat one placement after rotating the sensor antenna/body 90 degrees;
* record count, minimum, maximum, median, and interquartile range outside the
  callback; record queue drops and PHY/frame-class mix;
* repeat on C5 #1 at 2.4 GHz and C5 #2 at 5 GHz without comparing absolute
  values as though the radios were calibrated identically.

Provisional advance criteria for RSSI: no invalid/out-of-range values in valid
receptions; at least 95% sample availability; repeat placement medians within
10 dB under controlled unchanged conditions; and an ordered median shift across
near/middle/far in at least two of three adjacent comparisons. Failure does not
mean the radio is defective; it produces Experimental/Reject-for-purpose status.

For noise floor, require at least 95% availability and more than a constant
sentinel across deliberately different quiet/busy channel conditions before
acceptance. If it remains fixed while RF conditions change, classify Exposed
but insufficiently characterized. Per-frame versus window-summary value is a
v0.3.0 decision.

## 13. Timestamp and timing strategy

The public timestamp is an unsigned 32-bit local microsecond value. It wraps
every `2^32` microseconds: 4,294.967296 seconds, or 71 minutes 34.967296 seconds.
For intervals shorter than half the modulus, wrap-safe delta is unsigned
subtraction:

```c
uint32_t delta_us = current_timestamp - previous_timestamp;
```

No signed comparison of absolute timestamps is allowed. Discontinuities caused
by reset, Wi-Fi restart, channel transition, queue loss, or sleep must start a
new timing epoch. Discovery configurations should keep modem/light sleep off or
explicitly classify precision as degraded, matching the header warning.

Required tests:

* host vectors immediately before/after wrap, equal timestamps, and deltas near
  the half-range ambiguity boundary;
* controlled periodic traffic over several eight-second windows, comparing
  receive deltas with an independent host-side source at coarse tolerance;
* burst/idle traffic to establish whether short-window inter-arrival and activity
  timing are captured plausibly;
* one firmware run spanning the natural wrap if operationally practical, or a
  test seam that injects timestamps if not.

Acceptance for short-window timing requires monotonic wrap-safe deltas within a
capture epoch, no arithmetic failures at wrap, explicit drop/channel boundaries,
and at least 99% of controlled intervals within the tolerance justified by the
traffic generator and scheduler measurement. The exact tolerance must be set
before the run; do not retrofit it to results.

These timestamps are suitable candidates for local inter-frame timing and
bounded activity windows. They are not synchronized across C5s and must not be
used for cross-sensor ordering. Estimating offset/drift belongs to v0.4.0 unless
a small v0.2.0 feasibility measurement is needed to justify deferral.

## 14. Sequence, retry, fragment, and protected strategy

Sequence Control exists only when the selected layout includes it. Decode
fragment number from bits 0–3 and sequence number from bits 4–15 using explicit
little-endian access. Characterize sequences only within a controlled known
transmitter and compatible traffic context; sequence spaces and behavior can
depend on transmitter, traffic identifier, QoS, management/data context, reset,
and implementation.

The retry FC bit means the transmitter marked the MPDU as a retry. It does not
prove this sensor observed the earlier attempt, that two observations are safe
to deduplicate, or that RF quality is poor. A retry ratio is only an experiment
over eligible captured frames with drop rate and channel dwell stated.

The fragment number and More Fragments bit do not justify reassembly. They are
recorded only to establish availability and prevalence. The Protected bit says
the frame body uses protected-frame semantics; it does not identify the cipher,
prove successful encryption, or imply decryptability. BLACKSHEEP must never try
to decrypt or inspect application payload during this milestone.

Required evidence includes host bit-pattern fixtures, known management/data
sequences, controlled WPA2/WPA3 data showing the Protected bit, and an optional
degraded-link scenario that produces retries without specialized RF equipment.
No behavioral conclusion is embedded in parser output.

## 15. PHY and rate strategy

Scope should remain on documented public fields:

1. **Required:** inventory and physically exercise `cur_bb_format`; preserve
   `rate` as a raw conditional code with a validity tag.
2. **Investigate:** record whether public raw SIG words are nonzero under known
   HT/VHT/HE traffic and compare format classification with independently known
   AP/client settings.
3. **Gate before decoding:** produce a format-specific specification reference,
   golden vectors, and a demonstrated sensing question that decoded MCS/coding/
   GI/STBC would answer. Without all three, defer raw SIG decoding.
4. **Reject for this milestone:** private/internal ESP-IDF structures. Their ABI
   and semantics are not a reproducible public contract.
5. **Out of scope:** CSI. It is a separate high-volume subsystem and is not
   necessary to decide the current candidate attributes.

The likely disposition is Accept for PHY family with conditions, Experimental
for raw rate/SIG values, and Defer for decoded MCS/coding/GI/STBC. No value is
to be inferred merely from a bit position in a private source file.

## 16. Callback and queue architecture

### 16.1 Callback work

The callback performs only:

* null/type and success-state checks;
* a bounded normalization copy of documented RX-control values;
* conservative usable-length calculation;
* copy of `min(usable_length, 40)` payload-prefix bytes for non-MISC frames;
* a zero-wait enqueue into fixed internal RAM;
* atomic/saturating received, rejected, enqueued, and dropped counters.

It does not parse address semantics, print, allocate, look up OUI, retain
pointers, block, or touch PSRAM. If enqueue fails, it increments the drop counter
and returns immediately. The callback remains safe whether it runs in the Wi-Fi
task or a more restrictive future context.

### 16.2 Queue record and bounds

Conceptually:

```c
typedef struct {
    rx_metadata_snapshot_t rx;   /* selected public values, no packed alias */
    uint8_t callback_class;
    uint8_t copied_len;           /* 0..40 */
    uint8_t header[40];
} discovery_event_t;
```

Use 128 statically allocated entries initially. The exact compiled `sizeof`
must be asserted and reported; at an expected 64–96 bytes per event this costs
8–12 KiB plus queue bookkeeping. Allocate the queue storage in internal DRAM,
not PSRAM, because callback-critical access must not depend on external-memory
cache state. PSRAM remains available to the application but is unnecessary for
bounded discovery events.

Stage 1 establishes queue high-water and drop rate under representative and
stress traffic. Required steady-test target is below 1% drops with zero callback
blocking and no watchdog/panic; any nonzero drop is included in every summary.
If 128 entries cannot meet the target, first reduce diagnostics/worker cost and
filter scope. Increase capacity only after measuring internal-RAM margin and
documenting the new fixed bound.

### 16.3 Worker and shutdown

A dedicated worker blocks on the queue, calls the pure parser, updates fixed
counters/ranges/seen masks, and emits no per-packet logs. Summary output occurs
after the finite window. Shutdown order is: stop new promiscuous callbacks,
unregister callback, signal end-of-stream, drain or explicitly count discarded
queued events, join worker, print summary, stop/deinitialize Wi-Fi, then return
to READY/heartbeat. Counter widths use at least 64 bits in worker summaries or
saturate explicitly; overflow is reported.

Serial output is capped to one configuration header, aggregate summaries, and
a small controlled-identifier allowance. Timing/load runs may disable detailed
serial output entirely to avoid making instrumentation the bottleneck.

## 17. Data and privacy boundary

* Parse only the copied header prefix; never copy or inspect application payload.
* Do not retain raw callback buffers or pointers after callback return.
* Do not generate pcap or raw-frame dumps by default or in milestone acceptance.
* Keep queues, windows, counters, and diagnostic line counts bounded.
* Keep no persistent observation history or identifier database.
* Store no captures, SSIDs, information elements, or encrypted bodies.
* Ambient runs output aggregate counts and classifications only.
* Identifier-visible tests use owned/authorized known devices, a compile-time
  diagnostic gate, at most 16 printed addresses, and no file logging; the gate
  is off in default environments.
* Test documentation records scenarios and aggregate evidence, not identifiers.

The security policy applies to any accidentally captured sensitive material.
Test fixtures use synthetic or explicitly controlled addresses and contain no
real credentials or production identifiers.

## 18. Controlled test strategy

Ambient RF is useful for load and diversity but cannot establish semantics.

### Required without specialized RF equipment

* synthetic host fixtures for every parser layout and failure path;
* one known AP and one known client on 2.4 GHz and on 5 GHz;
* beacons/probe or association management traffic from controlled equipment;
* ordinary protected WPA2/WPA3 data traffic;
* broadcast and multicast traffic generated on the controlled network;
* a client using a known locally administered/randomized address, where the
  operating system permits explicit verification;
* fixed-channel windows for legacy plus at least one modern PHY format available
  from the equipment;
* repeated quiet, normal, and deliberately high-traffic windows;
* distance/orientation runs defined in the RF section.

### Useful optional tests

* RTS/CTS and Block Ack traffic if controllably produced;
* HT40 secondary-channel evidence;
* controlled attenuation/distance to elicit retries;
* four-address/WDS traffic from owned equipment;
* independently known fixed legacy rates and multiple HE traffic modes.

### Deferred to v0.7.0

Calibrated attenuation, anechoic testing, exhaustive PHY/rate coverage,
antenna radiation characterization, long-duration environmental qualification,
and production-level repeatability across a larger sensor population are not
v0.2.0 gates.

## 20. Validation layers

### 20.1 Static and host tests

Keep the parser independent of ESP-IDF so a native host compiler can test it.
Required suites:

* zero-through-maximum truncation at every field boundary;
* malformed/random input and unsupported type/subtype behavior;
* FC bit extraction, protocol/type/subtype, and callback-class mismatch;
* management, every supported control descriptor, data/QoS/HT-control, and
  four-address header lengths;
* all four To DS/From DS address-role mappings;
* sequence/fragment/retry/protected bit vectors;
* broadcast/multicast/local/global classification;
* channel/frequency mapping including unknown and boundary channels;
* unsigned timestamp wrap and epoch reset behavior;
* length-relation and copy-bound tests;
* queue overflow, saturation, and shutdown/drain state-machine tests;
* sanitizer-enabled randomized parser input where the host toolchain permits.

Golden fixtures must be synthetic or from controlled frames and contain no
ambient identifiers or payloads.

### 20.2 Firmware builds

Every new discovery environment and all v0.1.0 environments must clean-build.
For each, verify the native ESP-IDF CMake bootloader is generated, selected SHA
equals native SHA, and the regression guard reports ESP32-C5, DIO ROM-facing
header, 80 MHz, 32 MB, and revisions v1.0–v1.99. `git diff --check` and ignored-
artifact checks remain mandatory.

### 20.3 Physical tests

Test C5 #1 first for each shared stage; only proceed to C5 #2 after it passes.
Rediscover boards by USB serial and never commit COM ports. Each run verifies
bootloader, flash, PSRAM/memory test, Wi-Fi band/channel, bounded capture,
callback/queue/drop summary, clean shutdown, READY, and stable heartbeat without
panic/watchdog/reset/resource exhaustion.

### 20.4 v0.1.0 regression

Preserve normal bring-up, 2.4/5 GHz scans, passive validation, channel control,
and direct-metadata environments. Firmware work may reorganize pure reusable
code only with equivalent output/behavior and regression evidence; architecture
cleanup is not itself a milestone goal.

## 21. Recommended implementation stages

Each stage should be one reviewable local branch/commit after its acceptance
gate. Do not combine all discovery work into one firmware change.

### Stage 1 — capture contract and host-testable parser substrate

**Objective:** establish safe buffer ownership, bounds, FC primitives, and test
infrastructure before parsing real identifiers.
**Likely files:** new `firmware/esp32-c5/lib/wifi_frame_parser/` sources/headers;
new host tests under `tests/`; dedicated discovery environment/config; focused
documentation.
**Work:** define status/validity types; explicit LE readers; FC decode; type-
specific header-length calculator; 40-byte copy contract; 128-entry internal-
RAM queue prototype; instrument size/high-water/drop/callback duration without
packet printing. Resolve `sig_len`/`dump_len`/FCS copy bounds using known frames.
**Tests:** exhaustive truncation, randomized bytes under sanitizers, length
relations, queue overflow/drain, clean builds; physical fixed-channel load on
C5 #1 then C5 #2.
**Pass:** no out-of-bounds access; all invalid inputs bounded; no pointer escapes;
copy-length rule documented; below 1% drops in representative load with zero
blocking; stable shutdown/heartbeat.
**Dependencies:** v0.1.0 promiscuous environments.
**Non-goals:** semantic addresses or product records.
**Commit:** `Add bounded Wi-Fi header capture substrate`.

### Stage 2 — frame layout and packet-class validation

**Objective:** validate type/subtype and management/control/data header layouts.
**Likely files:** parser descriptor tables/tests; discovery worker summaries;
v0.2.0 evidence document.
**Work:** implement subtype-aware management/control/data descriptors, QoS,
four-address, and conditional HT Control lengths; compare parsed type with
driver callback class.
**Tests:** golden fixture per supported descriptor, every truncation boundary,
reserved subtypes, controlled management/data and observable control traffic on
both bands.
**Pass:** 100% golden mappings; malformed/reserved frames classified without
guessing; applicable real frames parse with class agreement and reported
unknown coverage.
**Dependencies:** Stage 1.
**Non-goals:** address meaning or aggregation.
**Commit:** `Characterize ESP32-C5 frame layouts`.

### Stage 3 — address roles and randomization classification

**Objective:** establish which raw and semantic address fields can be resolved.
**Likely files:** parser role resolver/tests; controlled diagnostic gate; evidence

**Work:** implement Addr1–4 presence, management/data mapping, subtype-specific

**Tests:** full DS matrix, WDS, short controls, local/global/group/broadcast

**Pass:** all host mappings exact; no OOB/missing-role guesses; driver group bit

**Dependencies:** Stage 2.
**Non-goals:** device identity, vendor attribution, retention.
**Commit:** `Characterize Wi-Fi address roles`.

### Stage 4 — sequence, retry, fragmentation, and protection flags

**Objective:** verify safe availability and limits of protocol-control attributes.
**Likely files:** parser/tests; worker counters; evidence docs.
**Work:** decode Sequence Control only where present and FC retry/protected/
fragment flags; keep context and validity tags.
**Tests:** bit-vector fixtures; known transmitter bursts; protected WPA2/WPA3
data; optional controlled retry/fragment traffic on both bands.
**Pass:** exact host decode; plausible controlled values; no claims of duplicate,
quality, cipher, or decryptability; unavailable physical cases marked
Experimental rather than manufactured.
**Dependencies:** Stages 2–3.
**Non-goals:** deduplication, reassembly, decryption, behavior analysis.
**Commit:** `Characterize Wi-Fi control attributes`.

### Stage 5 — RF context and length reliability

**Objective:** decide whether RSSI, noise, channel/frequency, band, and length
fields are reliable enough to advance.
**Likely files:** pure channel mapper/tests; fixed aggregation counters; evidence

**Work:** execute the near/middle/far/orientation matrix; validate channel against

**Pass:** predefined availability/repeatability criteria evaluated; every field

**Dependencies:** Stage 1; controlled traffic.
**Non-goals:** ranging, calibrated RF qualification, final aggregation.
**Commit:** `Characterize Wi-Fi RF attributes`.

**Provisional sequencing checkpoint:** Stage 5 implementation and controlled

### Stage 6 — local timing and activity feasibility

**Likely files:** pure timestamp helpers/tests; worker timing counters; evidence

**Work:** implement wrap-safe delta and epoch boundaries; run periodic/burst

**Tests:** wrap vectors and injected clocks; repeated both-band controlled runs;

## 22. Stage acceptance and evidence rules

Every stage must meet these common gates:

* host/static tests pass, including all newly introduced negative cases;
* clean firmware builds pass and the native bootloader SHA guard passes;
* callback work is bounded, non-blocking, allocation-free, and raw pointers do
  not escape;
* physical runs state band, channel, traffic source class, sample count, queue
  drops, parse failures, and duration;
* the board shuts down discovery cleanly and resumes stable READY/heartbeat;
* no firmware binary, capture, serial log, identifier, local path, or secret is
  committed;
* documentation distinguishes documented facts, project inference, and physical
  evidence;
* a failed criterion results in Experimental/Defer/Reject or an explicit blocker,
  not relaxed semantics after observing results.

## 23. Attribute acceptance framework

At Stage 9 each candidate receives exactly one disposition:

* **Accept:** repeatedly available in applicable controlled tests; semantics are
  documented and understood; extraction is bounded; useful context or
  differentiation is demonstrated; privacy handling is feasible.
* **Accept with conditions:** same, but valid only for named frame classes,
  layouts, PHY formats, bands, success states, or configured contexts. Validity
  must travel with the value in any later model.
* **Experimental:** plausible value but insufficient coverage, repeatability,
  semantics, or independent ground truth. It does not enter the default v0.3.0
  input set.
* **Defer:** technically exposed/derivable but current product value does not
  justify complexity, privacy cost, or test effort.
* **Reject:** unreliable or misleading, unbounded/too costly, dependent on
  private ABI, or not useful for BLACKSHEEP.

The final disposition ledger includes source, validity predicate, bands/frame
classes tested, sample/window counts, queue/drop conditions, cost, privacy
notes, unresolved questions, and rationale. “Observed once” is never sufficient
for Accept.

## 24. Failure and robustness model

| Condition | Required bounded behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Null callback buffer / unexpected class | Count and return; never dereference |
| MISC callback | Count class; copy/parse zero payload bytes |
| Failed RX state | Count; exclude from semantic parser unless a later explicit experiment enables it |
| Truncated header | Return exact truncation status; expose only fields already proven safe, no roles |
| Unknown protocol/type/subtype | Count unsupported; retain safe FC facts only |
| Missing address / ambiguous role | Validity false; never substitute zeros or guess |
| Inconsistent `sig_len`/`dump_len` | Use conservative copy bound; count discrepancy; no over-read |
| Callback-class/FC mismatch | Count mismatch; do not semantically process |
| Queue full | Drop newest event, increment saturating drop counter, return immediately |
| High traffic | Preserve bounded memory/work; filters and load shedding; report loss |
| Counter overflow | 64-bit or explicit saturation flag; never wrap silently |
| Timestamp wrap/reset | Unsigned delta within epoch; reset epoch at discontinuity |
| Unknown PHY/rate | Raw value plus invalid/unknown classification; no invented decode |
| Channel mismatch | Count and exclude from channel-specific conclusions |
| Shutdown with queued events | Disable source, then bounded drain or counted discard before freeing queue |

No malformed or adversarial frame may crash, panic, corrupt memory, block the
Wi-Fi callback, or cause unbounded diagnostics.

## 25. Risks and mitigations

Likelihood and impact are qualitative for planning.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation / decision trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Callback buffer lifetime misunderstood | Medium | High | Copy required fields/prefix synchronously; retain no pointer; confirm matching docs |
| Callback load/starvation | High | High | Fixed tiny copy, zero-wait enqueue, duration/high-water/drop instrumentation |
| Queue overflow in busy RF | High | Medium | Fixed 128 slots initially, drop-newest counter, reduce worker/log cost before resizing |
| `sig_len`/`dump_len` ambiguity causes over-read | Medium | High | Conservative minimum, known-length tests, fail closed until relation resolved |
| Address-role errors | Medium | High | Descriptor tables, full DS/control fixture matrix, unknown instead of guess |
| Control subtype edge cases | High | Medium | Subtype-specific minimums; unsupported status; no universal 24-byte layout |
| MAC randomization defeats identity/OUI assumptions | High | High | U/L and I/G gating; no identity claim; host-only OUI feasibility |
| Misleading OUI attribution | High | Medium | Eligible global individual addresses only; dataset/version caveats; defer database |
| RSSI/noise variability | High | Medium | repeated controlled placements, contextual results, no ranging claim |
| Timestamp wrap/sleep/reset | Certain/Medium | Medium | unsigned deltas, epoch boundaries, sleep-state record, wrap tests |
| Ambiguous public driver fields | High | Medium | conditional validity tags; retain raw values; Experimental/Defer disposition |
| Temptation to use private ESP-IDF data | Medium | High | public-API gate; reject without documented stability/value case |
| Incomplete PHY-format coverage | High | Medium | controlled known PHYs, explicit untested matrix cells, defer decoded PHY claims |
| Protected bit mistaken for decryptability | Medium | High | parser/documentation wording and no payload/decryption path |
| Memory pressure | Medium | High | internal-DRAM static queue, compile-time size assertion, heap margin measurement |
| Serial output distorts load/timing | High | Medium | summary-only output; disable detailed diagnostics during performance runs |
| ESP-IDF upgrade changes packed fields | Medium | High | pin 5.5.4, compile-time checks where valid, rerun inventory/tests on upgrade |
| Ambient traffic exposes identifiers | Medium | High | aggregate default, controlled-device gate, no logs/captures committed |

## 26. Open questions

1. What exact usable payload length does C5 promiscuous mode provide for each
   callback class, and why did v0.1.0 observe `dump_len = sig_len + 4`?
2. Does the driver include FCS bytes in the delivered C5 payload for each filter
   class, and can this change with aggregation or error filters?
3. Which control subtypes are delivered with the current filters on C5, and are
   their public buffer lengths complete enough for role parsing?
4. What do `rxmatch0..3`, nonzero receive-state values, and `sigb_len` mean for a
   passive STA-mode sensor beyond the limited public comments?
5. Does `noise_floor` update per frame or at another cadence, and does it respond
   usefully across quiet/busy controlled conditions?
6. Which legacy, HT, VHT, and HE formats can the available controlled equipment
   independently force on 2.4 and 5 GHz?
7. Are raw SIG words sufficiently documented and valuable to justify a separate
   public-spec decoder, or should they remain Experimental?
8. What queue high-water/drop behavior occurs in realistic dense RF, and is the
   initial 128-entry internal-RAM budget adequate?
9. Can controlled four-address, fragmentation, HT40, and retry scenarios be
   produced without disproportionate lab setup? If not, which remain host-only
   or Experimental?
10. How much useful manufacturer coverage remains after excluding local/group
    addresses, and is host/control-board lookup worth its lifecycle/privacy cost?
11. What tolerance is justified for periodic timing experiments given traffic-
    generator and host measurement error?
12. Does the public API explicitly guarantee callback execution context and
    buffer lifetime in documentation beyond the installed declarations? If not,
    maintain the conservative copy/no-block contract.

Unanswered questions are acceptable if their associated attribute is honestly
classified. They block v0.2.0 only when a target has no defensible disposition.

## 27. v0.2.0 completion gate

v0.2.0 is complete when:

* the safe parser/capture substrate and required discovery instrumentation exist;
* host negative/golden/wrap/mapping tests and firmware regression builds pass;
* both assigned C5 boards complete required controlled physical matrices;
* extraction cost, queue loss, validity, privacy, and band/PHY limitations are
  documented;
* every roadmap candidate attribute has one acceptance disposition;
* no unresolved question prevents an honest disposition;
* v0.3.0 has a defensible candidate input set.

Completion does not require a final sensor record, persistent aggregation,
simultaneous dual-C5 operation, control-board transport, REDLINE integration,
GNSS, or BLE. `VERSION` and release/tag decisions are separate release work.

## 28. Deferred v0.3.0 and later work

v0.3.0 will decide the structured observation model: field names and validity,
transient versus summarized data, bounded machine-readable output, address-role
representation, and whether accepted attributes become per-frame or aggregate
features. v0.4.0 handles dual-sensor operation and time/sequence coordination;
v0.5.0 implements local aggregation; v0.6.0 defines the control-board interface;
v0.7.0 performs broader sensor qualification. This brief supplies evidence and
decisions to those milestones but intentionally implements none of them.
